# Methods and interpretation

## Study domains

The monitoring domain is reproducible from the 1984–2021 JRC maximum surface-water extent. A broad Nevada–Arizona search window bounds the compute region. Starting in the western Boulder Basin core, the workflow traces connected historical water at 60 m and dilates it 180 m into a classification corridor. The core intersects a 90% water-occurrence mask. The window and core bounds are in [config.py](../src/lake_mead/config.py). The corridor can omit new water beyond its buffer or include tributary channels and shoreline infrastructure. Narrow causeways and water gaps can alter traced connectivity, so inspect both the search window and the corridor on the map.

The named Overton, Boulder and Virgin windows are geographic sampling windows intersected with the corridor and SAR shoreline mask. They approximate representative portions of the arms and basins rather than official basin boundaries. They are reported individually so a local delta, terrain void or radar geometry problem cannot silently stand in for the whole reservoir. Reclamation documents geomorphic change in the Overton Arm and an abrupt historical survey transition near 1,095 ft, supporting caution about transferring Overton performance to the rest of Lake Mead. A basin should be considered representative only after checking sample distributions, terrain support, inter-basin agreement and independent gauge comparison. [Reclamation survey report](https://www.usbr.gov/lc/region/g4000/LM_AreaCapacityTables2009.pdf)

## Observed extent

Earth Engine GRD already includes border and thermal noise removal, calibration and geometric terrain correction. IW images at 10 m catalog resolution are filtered by pass, relative orbit and VV or VH. Slices from the same platform and absolute orbit are mosaicked. The CLI chooses a single track by median search-window footprint overlap unless one is supplied. Platforms within a track are retained and recorded. The workflow screens the ellipsoidal incidence angle to 30–46°, applies a flat-surface gamma-nought approximation, then uses a 30 m radius median filter at 20 m grid spacing in UTM 11N. Residual relief-related radiometry, layover and shadow remain possible. Finer DEM cells cannot recover SAR information lost to resolution, filtering or geolocation. [Earth Engine SAR preprocessing](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/guides/sentinel1)

Otsu partitions filtered backscatter within the historical corridor. The inter-class variance fraction is recorded and very weak splits are rejected. Low-backscatter patches must contain at least nine eight-connected 20 m pixels and avoid steep valid terrain. Candidate water must also connect within 4 km of a historical persistent-water anchor that remains a candidate on the date. A 40 m routing grid uses any fine candidate per cell and gates the final 20 m pixels. It can bridge short gaps or omit water far from the nearest anchor. Original terrain masks are respected during elevation sampling, while missing terrain alone does not erase SAR water. Residual classification risks include windy water, smooth dry flats, urban surfaces and narrow arms. Defaults are starting settings and require independent optical or manually labeled shoreline checks.

The area report uses a fixed pixel grid and gives observed water, covered corridor and total corridor areas. Coverage below 95% excludes an acquisition. Above that floor the reported area remains conditional on the date's valid coverage. Changes on paired dates use the intersection of valid coverage, so missing data do not appear as gain or loss. For small trends, compare a fixed common footprint or explicitly propagate the unobserved area. Ordinary reservoir level fluctuations are described as inundation and exposure.

## Google GEM-15 terrain and quality proxies

Use the catalog collection `projects/climate-and-sustainability/assets/gem-15_v1`. The slash form `gem-15/v1` does not match Google's published collection identifier. The catalog reports local UTM 15 m pixels and EGM96 orthometric heights in metres (EPSG:5773). The bare-earth `dtm` is a learned product derived from optical stereo through 2024, primarily 2022–2024. The 2025 catalog timestamp is a dataset timestamp. Individual source acquisitions can have different pool levels. Preserve the native masks when assembling the UTM 11N mosaic. [Google catalog](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/projects_climate-and-sustainability_assets_gem-15_v1) · [GEM-15 preprint](https://eartharxiv.org/repository/view/16034/)

Water and a producer buffer of about 9 m are masked during upstream processing. Nearshore reclaimed land can remain blank where the stereo acquisition saw water. Conversely, currently inundated land may have a valid terrain value from a previous low pool. Low texture, shoreline mixed pixels, geometric offsets, image seams and shoreline erosion or deposition can create gaps or outliers. The masked DTM is not lake bathymetry. Its 15 m spacing does not demonstrate better shoreline elevation accuracy than a coarser product.

The sampler requires observed dry pixels at least 0.75 dB above the Otsu split, 25–65 m landward of connected water and on slopes at or below 12°. The broad 250–395 m EGM96 range guards against gross terrain artifacts. It brackets published reservoir operating levels (895–1,229 USGS 1912 ft) with margins for datum differences and landward rise. It is a plausibility screen, independent of the daily gauge series. The sampler uses the 15 m terrain grid and reports the DTM availability fraction of candidate land before slope and range screens. The terrain slope uses valid neighboring DTM cells, so the outermost valid cell along a void may still be excluded. The strip and slope can create an upslope bias. The nominal rise from the strip midpoint times tangent of the median sample slope is a sensitivity indicator. Local radar edges, slopes and voids may shift estimates in either direction. [Reclamation operating range](https://www.usbr.gov/ColoradoRiverBasin/post2026/draft-eis/docs/vol-1/P26-DEIS-3-Ch3.pdf)

The baseline estimates elevations from a seeded random sample of up to 1,500 eligible DTM pixels per basin. An alternative drops samples with unavailable quality diagnostics or large optical surface variability, using DSM standard deviation ≤2 m, P95−P05 ≤5 m and DSM−DTM ≤2 m. High values can reflect vegetation, fine relief or matching artifacts. These bands provide within-cell variation and surface-to-terrain differences, with no calibrated pixel-level DTM error band. The DSM P50 is available from Google for exploration. Compare screened retention, spatial footprint and gauge metrics on identical dates before claiming improvement. The thresholds have not been tuned with gauge data.

Within each window, the primary estimator is the median of 500 m cell medians after a robust outlier screen. The retained-cell lower quartile tests sensitivity to positive landward bias, while the raw sample median shows the influence of clustered samples. Estimates fewer than six retained blocks, above 8 m block IQR or with over 30% of blocks rejected fail QC. Block quartiles describe spatial dispersion. They are not a confidence interval or total uncertainty. Inspect terrain availability, sample support, dates, estimated cross-basin spread and radar quality alongside those numbers.

## Gauge validation, area–elevation and storage

RISE item 6123 contains provisional daily Hoover Dam observations in feet. The location metadata and Reclamation tables identify USGS 1912 (Power House Datum). RISE timestamps are converted to fixed MST and paired to the SAR sensing day. The gauge may represent a different time of day, and surface wind setup or flow gradients can yield differences across the reservoir. CSV import expects `date,elevation_ft` with ISO dates in fixed MST and USGS 1912 feet. The loader removes missing, invalid and implausible readings and rejects conflicting duplicate dates. [RISE item](https://data.usbr.gov/catalog/4370/item/6123) · [Datum metadata](https://data.usbr.gov/catalog/4370) · [API docs](https://data.usbr.gov/rise-api)

Feet are converted using 0.3048 m/ft. Unit conversion leaves the vertical datum unchanged. Without independent geodetic control, residuals and metrics are labeled unharmonized and include datum offset. If an independent transformation establishes a local shift, supply `--gauge-to-egm96-m` (signed shift added to gauge metres) and `--datum-source` (source and method). A Reclamation USGS 1912 to NGVD29 adjustment by itself does not transform to EGM96. Never infer a transformation by fitting the same dates used for accuracy assessment. Future calibration should lock a chronological holdout before estimator or threshold selection. The analysis has no fitted gauge correction and reports MAE, RMSE, signed bias and predictive R² where defined, with counts per domain and estimator.

Area–elevation points pair observed water area in square kilometres with terrain elevation estimates in metres EGM96. Use them as exploratory observations, with coverage and estimator QC retained. For storage, pass `--capacity-csv` with `elevation_ft,capacity_acre_ft` transcribed from the Reclamation version cited above. Confirm the chosen capacity column and convert thousands of acre-feet to acre-feet when transcribing. Storage is interpolated at official gauge levels in USGS 1912 feet, reports change from the first gauge row and omits values outside table support. The Sentinel-1 area and terrain scenario are independent of that capacity calculation.

## Connected terrain scenario

The notebook optionally shows land gain above a QC-passing baseline by starting from SAR-observed water. A cell is added only if observed dry, has a valid DTM value between 250 m and the specified EGM96 level, and falls within an 8 km raster path routed on a 45 m grid inside the historic corridor. It is a static connectivity scenario, with no travel time, hydraulics or levee breach analysis. Masked cells are barriers at the routing scale and may substantially underestimate gain. Channels and barriers narrower than the routing grid can disappear. The scenario cannot estimate pool drawdown or storage below the observed waterline.

## Verification priorities

Inspect the DTM void and variability layers on contrasting shoreline dates and assess whether omissions or DSM screening favor particular basins or water levels. Compare the landward strip, incidence angle and polarization choices using withheld dates and independent shoreline references. Where available, use local survey control and elevation profiles to characterize registration, temporal mismatch and vertical datum effects.
